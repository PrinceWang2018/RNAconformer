## What this shows

* The deep-learning and template-based methods get the global fold right on 8–10 of the 15 targets; RCV on none. RCV is a physics-inspired ensemble generator, not a competitor for single-structure accuracy.
* Most of the gap is the 3D arrangement of correct helices (junction geometry, coaxial stacking, tertiary contacts), which RCV does not model yet: on R1203, R1209, R1211 and R1296 RCV's base pairs are as accurate as AlphaFold 3's (INF-WC 0.86–0.98), but its TM-score is about half. Where ViennaRNA misses the 2D (pre-tRNA R1221s3/R1224s3, ZTP riboswitches R1263/R1264) the gap is larger.
* On the five targets that the field as a whole found hardest (CASP16 median model below TM-score 0.3: R1205, R1209, R1212, R1288, R1293) RCV is close to the median group and scores higher than AlphaFold 3 on three (R1209, R1212, R1288) — but at this level all predictions are essentially wrong.
* Practical advice: for one most-likely structure, use a deep-learning predictor and open its models in RCV; use RCV's own predictions for alternative folds, switching and pathways, which the single-structure tools do not provide.

## Notes

* CASP16 groups predicted blind in 2024 (many with templates, MSAs, deep learning and expert curation). RCV was run in 2026 from the sequence alone: ViennaRNA 2D hypotheses + physics-inspired 3D search, no templates, no MSA, no deep learning, nothing fitted to these targets. Its geometry parameters come from 178 PDB entries, none of which is a CASP16 target (checked by release date and sequence).
* RCV settings: 5 models, "thorough" sampling (40 trajectories), seed 42, run on a 16-core desktop CPU.
* TM-score above about 0.45 usually means the global fold is right; below 0.3 the fold is essentially random.
